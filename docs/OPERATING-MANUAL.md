# Operating manual

ShockAudit supports a researcher who already has a candidate policy change. Its output is a documented research design with linked sources, explicit choices, and unresolved questions. A selected policy or comparison is a research decision, not evidence that identification assumptions hold.

## 1. Your study and policy

Enter the policy name and the available study information. An official URL or identifier can help resolve an ambiguous request. Review the returned matches and confirm the intended instrument, jurisdiction, and episode. Later evidence work remains bound to that identity.

## 2. Understand the policy

The standard evidence review starts after policy confirmation. Review the overview, consolidated timeline, substantive change, coverage, and implementation information. Distinguish announcement, enactment, legal effect, compliance, and enforcement dates. Inspect sources and resolve consequential questions before relying on a factual interpretation.

## 3. Suggested Approach

Review the suggested comparisons in light of the intended population, outcome, period, and policy exposure. Select a comparison for review and examine the six design checks. Their assessments identify assumptions and evidence needs; they do not replace knowledge of the institutional setting or empirical diagnostics.

## 4. Check other events

Review the discovered events within the saved study scope. Discovery and assessment are separate steps. Request the relevance assessment to examine what an event is, why it may matter, and what to check next. Retain relevant possibilities, revise their interpretation, or proceed with none when appropriate. A related policy stage or downstream response is not automatically a confounder or control variable.

## 5. Compare explanations

Clarify the theoretical change, focal mechanism, and outcome. Review the proposed alternatives and their distinct processes, conditional directions, and distinguishing observations. The current model workflow supplies scholarly citation leads. Check those works independently before treating them as support for a substantive claim.

## 6. Design summary

Review the combined design record and diagram. Check that the policy episode, chosen comparison, concurrent events, and mechanisms reflect the intended study. Export the complete report in PDF or Word format. The report records design considerations and proposed checks; it does not report estimated effects or diagnostics that have not been run.

## Revising a study

Keep identity, factual support, comparison selection, and researcher judgment distinct. A material change to the study or policy can make earlier assessments stale. Review downstream content against the revised inputs before carrying it into a manuscript.

See the [evidence policy](EVIDENCE-POLICY.md) for source and claim boundaries and the [testing guide](TESTING.md) for the scope of automated verification.
